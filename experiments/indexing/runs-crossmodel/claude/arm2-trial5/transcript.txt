## Indexing — the part that matters at your scale

You said this runs in production against *several years of daily snapshots for hundreds of stores*, so `InventoryDaily` is the giant table (stores × products × days ≈ tens of millions of rows). Every index is tied to a query's access path:

- **`@@unique([snapshotDate, storeId, productId])`** — natural key (blocks duplicate snapshots) and, date-leading, serves **Page 4**'s per-store sum on one day.
- **`@@index([storeId, snapshotDate])`** — **Page 1**: find a store's latest date, then read that one store/date slice. No history scan.
- **`@@index([snapshotDate, isLowStock])`** — **Page 2**: pin the current date, then the low-stock flag.
- **`@@index([productId, snapshotDate])`** — product-across-stores lookups + the FK.
- **`@@index([storeId, productId, adjustedAt])`** on adjustments — **Page 3** end-to-end (match + time-ordered scan).
- **`Store.region`** index — the region filter that starts Pages 2 and 4.
- FK indexes throughout, since SQLite doesn't auto-create them and an unindexed FK turns a join into a table scan.

## Two interpretation calls I made (flag if you'd prefer otherwise)

- **Page 2 "low stock"** = low *on the latest snapshot date* (currently low), not "low at any point in years of history" — the historical set would be enormous and meaningless for a stock page.
- Both `getStoreCurrentInventory` and `getLowStockInRegion` do a cheap **two-step** (resolve the latest date, then read that slice) rather than a correlated subquery — it's simpler in Prisma and each step is a single indexed lookup.
