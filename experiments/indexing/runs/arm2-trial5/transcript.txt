**The scale decisions that shaped the code.** `InventoryDaily` is the only table that grows — years × daily × hundreds of stores × thousands of SKUs. Everything else is a dimension table. So:

- The unique key is `[snapshotDate, storeId, productId]`, which doubles as the access path for the regional roll-up. Two more composite indexes cover the store-centric pages (`[storeId, snapshotDate]`, `[storeId, snapshotDate, isLowStock]` — the boolean goes last because it's low cardinality and only trims an already-narrow store-day range).
- Pages 1, 2 and 4 are pinned to one snapshot date. Page 1 finds the store's latest date with an ordered `findFirst` on the store-leading index (stops after one row) rather than a `MAX()` over its history.
- **Page 2 is where I made a judgment call:** the spec doesn't mention a date, but "every product flagged low stock in a region" across years of daily snapshots returns the same SKU once per day it was ever low. I made `snapshotDate` optional and defaulted it to the region's most recent snapshot. Say the word if you actually want the historical form.
- Page 4 sums with `groupBy` in SQL — a region-day is hundreds of thousands of rows and none need to cross the wire. Pages 1–3 are paged and return a SQL `count` beside the rows.

**One assumption flagged in the README:** your model list gives `InventoryAdjustment` a "who adjusted it" field with no person model, so `adjustedBy` is a string and page 3 returns it directly. If adjusters are system users you'll want to filter and report on, that wants a `User` model with an FK — one migration, and the existing index doesn't move.
